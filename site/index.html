Erase a drive, then prove it.

ZeroTrace is a bootable USB stick. Pick a disk, and it erases it with the drive's own sanitize commands, reads it back to check the old data is gone, and hands you a signed certificate that anyone can verify.

Download the ISO Verify a certificate v0.1.0, 851 MB, early preview

Why

A lot of old laptops and drives end up in drawers instead of being reused, because their owners don't trust that wiping them actually works. They're often right to be suspicious. Deleting files or reformatting leaves the data recoverable, and overwriting an SSD from the outside can't reach all of its flash.

The usual tools either overwrite and hope, or erase properly but give you nothing to show for it. ZeroTrace does the erase the way the drive is designed to be erased, then gives you something you can hand to a buyer or a recycler.

How it works

  1. Boot it and pick a disk. The disk the USB booted from, and anything mounted or in use, is refused. You confirm by typing the drive's serial number.
  2. It erases with the drive's own commands where it can. NVMe Sanitize or Format, or ATA SANITIZE, tells the drive to destroy its internal encryption key or wipe its flash blocks itself. Drives that can't do that get a full overwrite instead.
  3. It checks the result. About a thousand random regions are read back from the disk itself, not from a cache. For firmware erases, random markers are written beforehand and every one of them has to be gone afterwards, so a drive that only claims to have erased gets caught.
  4. You get a certificate. A JSON file signed with Ed25519, recording the drive, the method, the result of the check and when it happened. Changing a single character breaks the signature.

Where it stands

This is an early preview. Please don't use it on a disk you care about yet.

  • Boots on UEFI and legacy BIOS machines, tested in VMs
  • Overwrite and NVMe Format erase, each followed by a read-back check
  • Signed certificates, saved to a USB stick labelled ZT-CERTS
  • NVMe Sanitize and ATA SANITIZE are written but not yet tried on real hardware, so they're never picked by default
  • An Android app for phones is planned

Download

Write the ISO to a USB stick with Rufus, balenaEtcher or dd and boot from it. To keep the certificates after you power off, plug in a second stick formatted FAT32 or exFAT and labelled ZT-CERTS.
